# Information Security Policy

Version: 3.1
Effective date: January 15, 2026
Owner: Chief Information Security Officer

## Purpose

This policy sets out how Quillmark Systems protects the information it holds and the platform it runs for its customers. It states the principles that every other security document builds on, names who is accountable for them, and explains how people at Quillmark learn what is expected of them, ask for exceptions, and raise concerns.

## Scope

The policy applies to everyone who works for or on behalf of Quillmark: employees, contractors, interns, and temporary staff. It covers information in every form, every system that stores, processes, or transmits it, and every place where work is done. Vendors that handle Quillmark information are covered through the Vendor Management Policy rather than directly by this document.

## Roles and responsibilities

This policy is owned by the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO). The CISO sets security direction, maintains the supporting policies and standards, and reports on the state of security to company leadership.

The Security Steering Committee meets quarterly and includes the CISO, the Chief Technology Officer, the VP of People, and the General Counsel. The committee reviews material risks, settles disagreements about priorities, and approves changes to security policy.

System owners are responsible for the security of the systems assigned to them, including who has access and how those systems are configured. Managers make sure the people they lead know and follow the policies. Every person at Quillmark is responsible for protecting the information they handle and for reporting anything that looks wrong as soon as they notice it.

## Policy

### Policy governance

All security policies are reviewed at least annually and approved by the Security Steering Committee. When a new risk cannot wait for that review, the CISO may issue interim guidance, and the guidance is folded into the next version of the policy it affects.

### Information classification

Quillmark classifies information into four levels: Public, Internal, Confidential, and Restricted. Public information has been approved for release outside the company. Internal information is meant for staff and contractors and would cause little harm if it leaked. Confidential information could damage the business or a partner if it were disclosed. Restricted information carries the strictest handling requirements and the heaviest consequences if it is exposed.

Customer data is always classified as Restricted. The owner of any other information may classify it at a higher level than its default, but never at a lower level than this policy requires.

### Security awareness

New employees and contractors must complete security awareness training within 30 days of their start date and every year after that. The training covers how to handle each classification level, how to recognize social engineering, how to report an incident, and the responsibilities set out in this policy. The People team tracks completion, and managers follow up with anyone who falls behind.

Phishing simulation exercises are sent to all staff monthly. The exercises are meant to teach rather than to punish. People who report a simulated message are recognized for it, and people who fall for it are offered coaching. Results are shared with the Security Steering Committee in aggregate form.

### Security exceptions

Exceptions to any security policy must be requested through the security exceptions process, approved by the CISO, and last no longer than 90 days, with at most one renewal. A request states which requirement cannot be met, why, what compensating controls will be in place, and when the underlying problem will be fixed. A renewal is considered only when the requester can show that remediation work is under way. An exception that is not renewed ends, and the original requirement applies again.

### Reporting concerns

Anyone who suspects a security incident, a lost device, or a breach of policy must report it to the Security team right away. Reports made in good faith are never held against the person who makes them. Reported incidents are handled under the Incident Response Plan.

## Enforcement and review

Violations of this policy may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment or contract. The response depends on the nature of the violation, whether it was deliberate, and the harm it caused or could have caused. An honest mistake that is reported promptly is treated differently from concealment or persistent disregard of the rules.

The Security team checks compliance through its own monitoring and through reviews in which system owners take part. The CISO reports the findings to the Security Steering Committee, which decides on any change to this policy when it carries out its review.
