# Vendor Management Policy

Version: 1.4
Effective date: March 1, 2026
Owner: Head of Procurement

## Purpose

This policy sets out how Quillmark chooses, onboards, monitors, and parts ways with the outside companies that supply it with software, infrastructure, and services. Vendors can handle Quillmark and customer data, or sit in the path of the platform itself, so their security has to be judged and watched in proportion to the risk they bring.

## Scope

The policy covers every outside company that provides goods or services to Quillmark and that touches company systems or data, or that the platform depends on to work. It applies to new vendors and to renewals of existing relationships, whatever the size of the contract. Contractors who work as individuals are covered by the Information Security Policy and the Access Control Policy.

## Roles and responsibilities

The Head of Procurement owns this policy and the vendor onboarding process. Procurement keeps the vendor inventory and the contract records. The Security team assesses vendors, assigns their risk tier, and reviews the evidence they supply. The General Counsel reviews contract terms. The business owner is the Quillmark team that wants to use the vendor; that owner explains what data the vendor will see and what the vendor will be used for, and is the contact for the relationship once it is running.

## Policy

### Risk tiers

Vendors are assigned one of three risk tiers. The Security team assigns the tier before a contract is signed, using what the business owner reports about the data and systems involved.

Tier 1 vendors have access to Restricted data or are critical to delivering the Quillmark platform.

Tier 2 vendors have access to Confidential data.

Tier 3 vendors have no access to non-public Quillmark data.

When a vendor fits the description of a higher-risk tier as well as a lower-risk tier, the higher-risk tier applies. A change in what a vendor does for Quillmark, such as a new integration with customer data, prompts the Security team to reconsider the tier.

### Due diligence

Before a contract is signed, the vendor completes a security questionnaire sized to its tier, and the Security team reviews the answers and any supporting evidence. Tier 1 vendors must provide a SOC 2 Type II report or an ISO 27001 certificate before a contract is signed and every year after that. The Security team reads the report or certificate for gaps that matter to the way Quillmark uses the vendor and records its conclusions. A vendor with serious unresolved gaps does not go forward until they are fixed or an exception is approved through the security exceptions process.

### Contract requirements

Contracts with vendors that handle Quillmark data state the security obligations the vendor accepts. Vendors must notify Quillmark of any security incident affecting Quillmark data within 24 hours of discovering it. The notice goes to the Security team, which treats it as a reported incident under the Incident Response Plan. Contracts also set out the vendor's duties when the relationship ends.

### Ongoing monitoring

Vendor risk is reassessed annually for Tier 1, every two years for Tier 2, and at contract renewal for Tier 3. A reassessment looks at the vendor's current evidence, any incidents it has reported, and any change in how Quillmark uses it.

Vendor user accounts in Quillmark systems are included in the quarterly user access review. System owners remove vendor accounts that are no longer needed, and the removal does not wait for the review if the work has ended.

The vendor inventory is owned by Procurement and reviewed quarterly by the Security team. The review checks that every active vendor is listed, that its tier is still right, and that vendors no longer in use have been closed out.

### Offboarding

When a vendor contract ends, the vendor must return or destroy Quillmark data and certify the destruction within 60 days. Procurement keeps the certification with the contract record. The business owner confirms that the vendor's access to Quillmark systems has been removed, and the Security team checks that the removal is complete.

## Enforcement and review

Using a vendor that has not gone through this process is a breach of policy. The Head of Procurement and the Security team work with the business owner to bring the relationship into line or end it, and the Information Security Policy governs any disciplinary response.

This policy is reviewed on the schedule set by the Information Security Policy, and changes are approved by the Security Steering Committee.
